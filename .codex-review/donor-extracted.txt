May 2013The above subject Trust was formed in 2006. It was inspired by a child called Mpho, who was born without limbs and who became the first beneficiary to be given artificial legs.Below is the background on Mpho Madi:Mpho Thembelihle Madi was born December 1 2000 in Durban. Mpho is bubbly, confident and full of zest for life. She was born without upper and lower limbs. Although Mpho does not have limbs she attained her milestones like any able-bodied child would, and remarkably started walking at 12 months.Mpho started her schooling years at The Open air school in 2004. The school regulations were that they did not admit children below the age of five for preschool. Mpho was invited for an assessment and  impressed  the teachers  with her bubbly personality , intelligence ,  zest for life as well as her supreme confidence, she was then admitted to the  preschool and achieved success in swimming ,garnering the  most improved swimmer  award  that year.In 2006 our family  moved back to Kwa thema and re-established our roots in our home town However , we  experienced problems finding a suitable school for our little angel  , We attempted to get her  into Muriel Brand , a school for the disabled . They, however, were reluctant to   accept her, as they believed that that their school environment would not be conducive to developing a child of her immense potential. Specify Pretoria school accepted her even though they felt that she did not belong in the school as it catered for remedial learning. At the beginning of 2007 Mpho was accepted at Muriel Brand in Brakpan even though the principal believed that the school was not suitable for Mpho
s intellectual growth and would in fact be a hindrance to her progress...Mpho is currently in Grade 4 where she excels in her favourite sport of swimming having already won an award. She is also loves music and dances energetically to her favourite tunes.Everybody that interacts with this wonderful little girl is immediately won over by her charm, intelligence, exuberance and friendliness. Apart from being a caring loving child she is also a single-minded, strong personality that is not easily intimidated by the challenges of everyday life...On 4 November 2008, Mpho received her lower limbs. The Prosthetic knees incorporated into the Prosthesis for each lower limb gave her the ability to walk independently. At present she is using a walking frame, but is progressing admirably. 
Update on Mpho May 2013 and School progressMpho has been going for physiotherapy once a week for the past year. She has improved as she is now able to stand without the assistance of the walking frame.Below is a picture of Mpho in physio standing without the frame. Mpho can also now walk at least 400m unassisted and her balance is more stable.  She walks to the park and back home at least once a week.Her physio has mentioned that she can benefit more from attending more than one physio sessions per week.Mpho is two years behind her peers, mainly because the school felt her intellectual ability was not at par. However, as a parent I felt it was not the correct reporting of her ability. As a family we took a decision to give her extra lessons at home and made her write the ANA test and we approached the school with her results but the school did not move her. The matter ended up with the Department of Education. I must commend the prompt response from the department. Mpho was moved from skills class to mainstream grade 4. We agreed to place her in grade 4 in February 2013 as it was her first exposure to mainstream education. I am pleased to say she passed her first term with an average of 47.5% with only two months exposure. With extra support and more exposure she can achieve better results. Her recent results show improvement with Afrikaans at 72% NS+T 51.5%. I am confident that her midterm results will be much better.Although we can't catch up with the two years lost in the discussions of her progress, Mpho can still be anything she wants to be in the future. This led to me as a parent posting an update on facebook " we should not limit our children to anything as we don't fully understand what they are capable of" being disabled does not mean one is unable to do things other people do they just do them differently.
